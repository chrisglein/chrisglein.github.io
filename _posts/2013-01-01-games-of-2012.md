We often bemoan the encroaching invasiveness of the modern digital age.&nbsp; Services like Facebook and Google are pretty notorious for collecting all kinds of data on us.&nbsp; The amount of information that they know about a single person can be a bit creepy.&nbsp; There's a fair amount of faith required to trust that they will respect our privacy, and it's clear that some are better at this than others.&nbsp; But there are times when I really appreciate my digital footprint.&nbsp; I like being able to explore my memory with the aid of some automatic stenographer in the cloud.&nbsp; It's with this help that with a quick run through of Xbox and BoardGameGeek that I can tell you exactly what I played in 2012.
<!--more-->

So, if you didn't already know it, I'm a gamer.&nbsp; Hello.&nbsp; It's pretty irrefutable when you look at these lists below.&nbsp; I enjoy games at some fundamental level.&nbsp; I love learning.&nbsp; I love skill mastery.&nbsp; I love interactive narrative.&nbsp; I love the breath of experiences that games can often.<br />
<br />
As various publications out there are putting together "game of the year" lists I feel like this is a good time to reflect back on my year of gaming.&nbsp; Outside of <a href="http://chrisglein.blogspot.com/2012/02/bastion.html">Bastion</a> I didn't make any game-related posts in 2012 (finishing the <a href="http://chrisglein.blogspot.com/search/label/musicmademe">Music Made Me</a> project took at the words out of me).&nbsp; I'm not going to be able to comment on this entire backlog, or cough up some grandiose meta-editorial last minute.&nbsp; But I can list them; that seems like a thing I can do.<br />
<br />
