One interesting aspect of the new <a href="http://armory.worldofwarcraft.com/">Armory</a> feature for <span class="blsp-spelling-error" id="SPELLING_ERROR_0">WoW</span> is that you can now go look up <a href="http://armory.worldofwarcraft.com/?#character-sheet.xml?r=Destromath&n=Ichigo">that bastard</a> who corpse camped you for half an hour.  Now I know that I was only one of 49514 (Contrast that with <em>my</em> lifetime kill count of 2372, which unfortunately I was never able to add that bastard to).
<!--more-->

I play on a <a href="http://www.wowwiki.com/PvP"><span class="blsp-spelling-error" id="SPELLING_ERROR_1">PvP</span></a> server because quite frankly it makes everything just a tad more interesting.  I'm one of those people that actually enjoyed <a href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultima_online"><span class="blsp-spelling-error" id="SPELLING_ERROR_2">Ultima</span> Online</a>'s <span class="blsp-spelling-error" id="SPELLING_ERROR_3">PvP</span> system.  Nothing made your heart beat quite as fast as having the bounty board's number one menace riding towards you.  Likewise with <span class="blsp-spelling-error" id="SPELLING_ERROR_4">WoW</span>, the <span class="blsp-spelling-corrected" id="SPELLING_ERROR_5">possibility</span> of random <span class="blsp-spelling-error" id="SPELLING_ERROR_6">PvP</span> adds a wild card into what could otherwise just be a standard <span class="blsp-spelling-error" id="SPELLING_ERROR_7">RPG</span> grind.<br /><br />However, <span class="blsp-spelling-error" id="SPELLING_ERROR_8">PvP</span> is only really fun when it's relatively even.  And unfortunately the design goals of the endless <span class="blsp-spelling-error" id="SPELLING_ERROR_9">MMORPG</span> gear grind are at odds with the design goals of fair <span class="blsp-spelling-error" id="SPELLING_ERROR_10">PvP</span>.  Even <span class="blsp-spelling-error" id="SPELLING_ERROR_11">WoW's</span> reward for <span class="blsp-spelling-error" id="SPELLING_ERROR_12">PvP</span> is just <a href="http://www.worldofwarcraft.com/pvp/rewards-general-armor.html">more gear</a>.  Which is a bulletproof "rich get richer" game design.  That would maybe work if the <span class="blsp-spelling-error" id="SPELLING_ERROR_13">PvP</span> matchmaking brackets were based on gear value, rather than the mostly useless measure of Level (given that there's a level cap and <span class="blsp-spelling-error" id="SPELLING_ERROR_14">everyone's</span> at it).  Supposedly they've fixed that and added a better matchmaking system for the <a href="http://www.worldofwarcraft.com/pvp/arena/index.xml">Arenas</a>, but those aren't "casual" friendly as they require you to form a regular team of consistent players.  Essentially leaving no real viable outlet for the occasional <span class="blsp-spelling-error" id="SPELLING_ERROR_15">PvPer</span>.<br /><br />But this is all part of the appeal of <span class="blsp-spelling-error" id="SPELLING_ERROR_16">MMORPGs</span>, I guess.  In a <span class="blsp-spelling-error" id="SPELLING_ERROR_17">singleplayer</span> <span class="blsp-spelling-error" id="SPELLING_ERROR_18">RPG</span>, you're always the hero.  You rise up and save the world.  You're special.  But in an <span class="blsp-spelling-error" id="SPELLING_ERROR_19">MMORPG</span>, you're competing against thousands of other potential heroes just like you.  Which means you're guaranteed to be outclassed by a large percentage of them, making your digital self just as mundane as your physical one.
