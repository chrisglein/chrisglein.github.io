I've been having a good time with <a href="http://chrisglein.blogspot.com/2008/01/last-night-on-earth.html">Last Night on Earth</a>, but poking around on <a href="http://www.boardgamegeek.com/">BoardGameGeek</a> I've always been a little jealous of those swank miniatures that some people have <a href="http://www.boardgamegeek.com/image/348631">painted</a>. I've also noticed that it's very possible to confuse the heroes while playing, so the painted figured do serve some functional value. So I decided to take up an arts and crafts project to paint the pieces from my game. 
<!--more-->

<p align="center"><a href="https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEg7Dv4Dz7OYe_t0GEa3R-6gHqzcDFivL-nWBQTlbGB2_fZW2quOmu63t-GFg2qwfQWVs3vufKS_3GRtk-2gnJ4eQsxXMMKW85eTRuLvCkwJlvtGBMtX0RD6zAnF-WbilUs0ldKoBgrLWD8/s1600-h/ZombiePainting-2.jpg"><img id="BLOGGER_PHOTO_ID_5228332092304450418" style="CURSOR: hand" alt="" src="https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEg7Dv4Dz7OYe_t0GEa3R-6gHqzcDFivL-nWBQTlbGB2_fZW2quOmu63t-GFg2qwfQWVs3vufKS_3GRtk-2gnJ4eQsxXMMKW85eTRuLvCkwJlvtGBMtX0RD6zAnF-WbilUs0ldKoBgrLWD8/s320/ZombiePainting-2.jpg" border="0" /></a></p><br /><br /><div align="left"></div><div align="left">I've never painted anything like these soft plastic miniatures before, so <a href="http://www.boardgamegeek.com/article/2485965#2485965">I posted a query</a> to the BGG crowd for tips. And I'm so glad I did, because those people were ridiculously helpful. Armed with pages of discussion on optimal painting techniques I headed to <a href="http://www.michaels.com/">Michael's</a> to get my materials.<br /></div><div align="left"></div><p align="center"><a href="https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEhQARAqRLlr05_ah8nsc7mnsaBEw5M8tzBm-0MimPrejDul7bjddTak1DIp-QGXAt87TP6S8Ro9rtp_rv-0wbDGcFXc5e5hT2fVYxm-tDYwPXbft0kEVfUYVsCC4WjR3-_SFvykgXaF_ao/s1600-h/ZombiePainting-4.jpg"><img id="BLOGGER_PHOTO_ID_5228333252453725506" style="CURSOR: hand" alt="" src="https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEhQARAqRLlr05_ah8nsc7mnsaBEw5M8tzBm-0MimPrejDul7bjddTak1DIp-QGXAt87TP6S8Ro9rtp_rv-0wbDGcFXc5e5hT2fVYxm-tDYwPXbft0kEVfUYVsCC4WjR3-_SFvykgXaF_ao/s320/ZombiePainting-4.jpg" border="0" /></a></p><div align="left">Supply list: </div><ul><li>Apple Barrel 24 color acrylic paint set - $4.99 </li><li>Apple Barrel white, 2oz - $0.79 </li><li>Apple Barrel black, 20z - $0.79 </li><li>Delta Ceramcoat matte interior varnish - $2.29 </li><li>Loew-Cornell set of four sable brushes - $6.99 </li><li>Elmer's adhesive putty - $1.99 </li><li>Solo bathroom cups (package of 80) - $2.39 </li></ul>Total: $21.88 (including tax)<br /><br />The first step was to wash the minis. Apparently they have some residual stuff on them from the mold, and if you don't wash them the paint will have problems sticking. So I washed all the minis and left them to dry for a couple hours.<br /><br /><div align="center"><a href="https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEjHjHL3QIfOpsWizveJdSJTUdmcObJE0gUdliSaGMGhrfbA704N2y7pwgwIdEaja1yaC6EOQ-VojXSBT8wE-xE6i3KhrmY1SPmSISrTOylEJeieDi723xpvQcUdpXRxYdWo2n972UZLPlI/s1600-h/ZombiePainting-5.jpg"><img id="BLOGGER_PHOTO_ID_5228332313373321042" style="CURSOR: hand" alt="" src="https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEjHjHL3QIfOpsWizveJdSJTUdmcObJE0gUdliSaGMGhrfbA704N2y7pwgwIdEaja1yaC6EOQ-VojXSBT8wE-xE6i3KhrmY1SPmSISrTOylEJeieDi723xpvQcUdpXRxYdWo2n972UZLPlI/s320/ZombiePainting-5.jpg" border="0" /></a><br /></div><br /><br /><div align="left"></div><div align="left"></div><div align="left">The next step was to prime them.  I started with the zombies, because you've gotta screw up pretty fierce to make zombies look bad.  Two coats of black paint later, my zombies were looking like freaky little tar monsters. </div><p align="center"><a href="https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEh7E_BaOZfBRqdGfU0AATWd8ml8EQcrWNlAiWVlo7AFzObzDf9U-jstz57ddZY4b1wf7TDEns4lLOs9fk4mXzaFkR4qb9zo9oshDUfiWA7Dt9NOD2QxfDiMGQb-xCfBDkaxGwvycKCCfds/s1600-h/ZombiePainting-10.jpg"><img id="BLOGGER_PHOTO_ID_5228331946174517922" style="CURSOR: hand" alt="" src="https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEh7E_BaOZfBRqdGfU0AATWd8ml8EQcrWNlAiWVlo7AFzObzDf9U-jstz57ddZY4b1wf7TDEns4lLOs9fk4mXzaFkR4qb9zo9oshDUfiWA7Dt9NOD2QxfDiMGQb-xCfBDkaxGwvycKCCfds/s320/ZombiePainting-10.jpg" border="0" /></a></p><div align="left"></div><div align="left">Someone from the boards had the excellent suggestion of using sticky tack to affix the figures to something so I didn't have to actually touch the figure while painting. Thus the plastic cups. There are two colors of zombies, green and brown, and since I needed to preserve that in my final output I marked the cups with either a "G" or "B".<br /></div><p align="center"><a href="https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEiCrjcCIiM2PaLZOmuCNVRpeF0-zU8vYemEgcLWsRQvbkdzikGnFouqClbutiwpAxaFva-U8iVZ_J0_ziBKFK3BrINeyAI3SqhubRPBp_6F32J8AKczFhRRwpP9SmmqlqkEGpHw2R5KxPo/s1600-h/ZombiePainting-9.jpg"><img id="BLOGGER_PHOTO_ID_5228333894832426994" style="CURSOR: hand" alt="" src="https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEiCrjcCIiM2PaLZOmuCNVRpeF0-zU8vYemEgcLWsRQvbkdzikGnFouqClbutiwpAxaFva-U8iVZ_J0_ziBKFK3BrINeyAI3SqhubRPBp_6F32J8AKczFhRRwpP9SmmqlqkEGpHw2R5KxPo/s320/ZombiePainting-9.jpg" border="0" /></a></p><div align="left"></div><div align="left">With all the prep complete it was time to break out the color. I wanted to preserve the original skin tone difference between the two sets of zombies, so I mixed two not-quite-human skin colors. After the skin was painted on zombies I was able to go nuts applying various clothing colors to them, making full use of the 24 colors in my palette. Lastly I finished off the bases, starting with a dark-green base (to match the game board) and then accenting them with either a light green or brown to bring back the two zombie team colors.<br /></div><div align="center"><br /><a href="https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEjwSM4VXpLnnHgU3CQwXZme0Sxiv3peTE4gH9eaTdEiHew8oi5nz2HpvgboDVT_NYFKTuSXfHuPmKOeuH5yd_13UglIUWTrMSaDovRM_q_5VSrBeuCeabDITP4-U6JBgS3EZ_VJhDIS01M/s1600-h/ZombiePainting-6.jpg"><img id="BLOGGER_PHOTO_ID_5228331724559471218" style="CURSOR: hand" alt="" src="https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEjwSM4VXpLnnHgU3CQwXZme0Sxiv3peTE4gH9eaTdEiHew8oi5nz2HpvgboDVT_NYFKTuSXfHuPmKOeuH5yd_13UglIUWTrMSaDovRM_q_5VSrBeuCeabDITP4-U6JBgS3EZ_VJhDIS01M/s320/ZombiePainting-6.jpg" border="0" /></a></div><br /><br /><div align="left"></div><div align="left">The final step was to apply a coat of matte varnish to make sure that the paint won't wear down as the figures get handled during gameplay.<br /></div><div align="left"></div><p> </p><p><a href="http://www.flickr.com/photos/chrisglein/2715341435/"><img style="DISPLAY: block; MARGIN: 0px auto 10px; WIDTH: 400px; CURSOR: hand; TEXT-ALIGN: center" alt="" src="http://farm4.static.flickr.com/3210/2715341435_cf1d92f5ec_o.jpg" border="0" /></a><br /></p><div align="left">You can see more photos of the final result <a href="http://www.flickr.com/photos/chrisglein/tags/zombie/">here</a>.
