A few years ago, during the short but always slightly chaotic drive to elementary school drop-off, I started telling my daughter about music—not just what I liked, but where it all *came* from.

I’d taken a college course once called **The History of American Popular Music**, and it stuck with me more than most classes ever did. Recently, I found the old syllabus again. I even tracked down the original textbook in a used bookstore. So I thought: what if I revisited the course? And what if I shared it—*expanded it*—with my daughter in bite-sized form during our morning commutes?

### 🚗 From Commute Chat to Blog Project

At first, it was just for us. A little ritual. A new genre or era each week, with some tracks to listen to and a quick story to go with them. But when I started telling other people about it, something surprising happened:  
**A lot of adults lit up.**

Turns out, *a ton* of people feel like they missed the part where music history gets explained. Where you learn how jazz turned into rock, or what field recordings sounded like, or why Motown mattered. So now I’m turning our commute project into a blog project—open to anyone who’s curious about where music comes from and where it’s been.

And I’ve been loving the research. Going deeper than the syllabus ever did. Pulling in other sources. Finding songs I’d never heard or never listened to *closely*. This series is a way to share that delight.

## 📚 What This Is

Each post will move **chronologically** through the history of recorded American music, starting in the late 1800s and working forward. Most posts will focus on a few songs at a time—usually five or fewer—with:

- 🎧 A **Spotify embed** for each track  
- 📝 A short write-up with history, context, and personal commentary  
- 🔗 Optional extra tracks for deeper listening

This isn’t meant to be definitive. It’s personal, a little playful, and full of curiosity. Some tracks will be important milestones. Others will just be songs I find fascinating. If you’ve ever thought, *I kind of want a guided tour of American music*, this is for you.
