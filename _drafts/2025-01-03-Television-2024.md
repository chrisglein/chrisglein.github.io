Continuing on from my [2023 coverage of television](https://chrisglein.com/2023/12/30/Television-2023.html), below you'll find the shows I watched in 2024 that felt like that warranted a mention.

## [X-Men 97](https://ondisneyplus.disney.com/show/x-men-97)
[![X-Men 97](/media/posts/x-men-97.jpeg)](https://ondisneyplus.disney.com/show/x-men-97)
My and my daughter led up to this by watching all 5 seasons of the original X-Men animated series from the 90s. Which was an uneven experience, but honestly better than I feared and my daughter really enjoyed it. Most of the classic X-Men storylines are covered here, and this led us to also explore the comics and movies together which was really fun. It was a good time right until the end of the animated series' run where there were sudden changes of voice actors and a severe degradation of art quality before a sudden end. Yikes it ended in a rough way. Thankfully, almost 30 years later the show was picked up to continue with a new season. And that season is great. I have no idea if it's something you can jump into without the context of everything that preceded it. But being confused about history you didn't catch up on is part of the experience of comics? If you like the X-Men, I'd recommend this one regardless.

## [Invincible](https://www.primevideo.com/detail/Invincible/0K677J96WQ96K6UY6BL15O70CO)
[![Invincible](/media/posts/invincible.jpg)](https://www.primevideo.com/detail/Invincible/0K677J96WQ96K6UY6BL15O70CO)
It's widely agreed that we exist in a time of superhero fatigue. It's interesting in this time to go back and watch the Marvel movies with my daughter, and realize that when the movies are good there is no fatigue. Good superhero stories are still good. Invincible is absolutely a show I cannot watch with my daughter. This is an adult take on superhero stories, with moments of particularly acute violence and gore. Which is going to immediately draw comparisons to [The Boys](https://www.amazon.com/The-Boys-Season-1/dp/B0875L45GK), particularly with a "what if Superman but bad?" plotline. I found Invincible to have just so much more _heart_ than The Boys (which was so bleak that I had to tap out) or Robert Kirkman's other successful comic/television creation [The Walking Dead](https://imagecomics.com/comics/series/the-walking-dead). This show knows how to have a poignant moment with well-paired music. It also indulges in "superheroes are wacky" vibes that evoke [Rick and Morty](https://www.adultswim.com/videos/rick-and-morty) or [The Venture Bros](https://www.adultswim.com/videos/the-venture-bros/). It is creatively referential to other famous superhero stories (e.g. "Monster Girl", who is basically the Hulk but gets younger every time she transforms). It's playful, it's heartful, it's sometimes extremely violent, but it also has something more to say than "let's celebrate adult nihilism."

## [Mr. and Mrs. Smith](https://www.amazon.com/gp/video/detail/amzn1.dv.gti.b69a9b54-0714-477a-88b5-4646861a2b7d)
[![Mr. and Mrs. Smith](/media/posts/mr-and-mrs-smith.jpg)](https://www.amazon.com/gp/video/detail/amzn1.dv.gti.b69a9b54-0714-477a-88b5-4646861a2b7d)
This show is so much more interesting than the movie that spawned it, which was a competent flick of beautiful spies trying to blow each other up. Instead this show has time to explore the strange dynamic of two people thrown together to have a mock relationship while holding a secretive and dangerous job. It’s a great setting to tell what is at its core a grounded relationship story.

## [The Entire History of Video Games](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=argpSxB1NQE)
[![The Entire History of Video Games](/media/posts/the-entire-history-of-video-games.jpg)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=argpSxB1NQE)
This isn't split up into episodes on YouTube, but as a 6 hour video you're certainly not going to consume it in one sitting. I watched this with my daughter and she found it incredibly interesting. The video walks through key moments in video game history from the very beginning to modern times. The narrator is a bit dry, but the content is interesting enough to stand on its own. Whether it's a trip through nostalgia or learning something new, this is a good watch if you're interested in the subject matter.

<p class="playLine"><span class='playIcon youtube'></span>Watched on <a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=argpSxB1NQE">YouTube</a></p>


## [The JHS Show](https://www.youtube.com/@jhspedals)
[![The JHS Show](/media/posts/jhs-show.png)](https://www.youtube.com/@jhspedals)
Not strictly speaking a television show, but rather a YouTube channel. One that covers guitar pedals. I found it in my [journey to my first pedalboard](https://chrisglein.com/2024/07/28/My-First-Pedalboard.html), but then continued to absolutely devour their many years backlog of content. The primary reason for the channel is that JHS is a pedal manufacturer, and they promote their own pedals on the show. But they also just as enthusiastically celebrate other manufacturer's pedals, and particularly the history of evolution of guitar effects. I've learned a ton from the channel. And yes, it has persuaded me to make many more purchases of both JHS pedals and other pedals (landing my [much larger pedalboard](https://chrisglein.com/2024/08/08/Pedalboard-Version-2.html)).

<p class="playLine"><span class='playIcon youtube'></span>Watched on <a href="https://www.youtube.com/@jhspedals">YouTube</a></p>


## [Arcane](https://www.netflix.com/title/81435684)
[![Arcane](/media/posts/arcane.webp)](https://www.netflix.com/title/81435684)
Okay, if I’m being honest I’d already seen this, but the introduction of a second season (which I haven’t started yet) prompted me to rewatch the first season with my partner. And damn this show is good. The quality of the art direction is phenomenal. The animation, particularly of the character driven fight scenes, is on its own level. This is one of the best things you can put in your eyeballs. I'm eager to start season 2.

## And the Rest

Here are some other shows that are worth a little mention.

#### Maybe Yes

- [House of the Dragon: Season 2](https://www.hbo.com/house-of-the-dragon): The intrigue continues to build this one, and holy crap there are some epic dragon-fueled moments. At this point I can upgrade this show from "maybe this is going to be okay" to "I'm enjoying this."
- [You: Seasons 1 and 2](https://www.netflix.com/title/80211991): This show tells the story of a man's inappropriate obsession with a woman, his deception to woo her, and you waiting for all the lies to inevitably come crashing down in the end. It's at times uncomfortable to watch. And at other times you find yourself empathizing with this guy who has done horrific things to maneuver into someone's life. We tend to root for our protagonists, and forget for a moment that no they do not deserve to come out ahead. There's more to this show but 2 seasons was enough intentional discomfort, at least for now.
- [True Detective: Night Country](https://www.hbo.com/true-detective/season-4/1-night-country-part-1): This murder mystery takes place in a frigid sunless Alaska. Jodie Foster absolutely crushes it as a self-destructive small town sheriff. The mystery feels mystical but stays just this side of realism. Somewhat like shadows feeling more sinister when you're sleep deprived and cold. It's good.
- [Silo: Season  1](https://www.primevideo.com/detail/Silo/0GKY3CQGOYOPDSE76BWMJN5CK3): I'm in the middle of reading book 3 of this series. And we get a television adaptation that's quite good. It's a bit funny to have both this and [Fallout](https://www.amazon.com/Fallout-Season-1/dp/B0CN4GGGQ2) in the same year, just to make sure we have enough stories about what happens when civilization goes into a bunker for generations. Silo however is the story of a mystery and rebellion. I enjoyed the first season of it and am eager to delve into the rest.
- [Fallout](https://www.amazon.com/Fallout-Season-1/dp/B0CN4GGGQ2): By all rights this show should have been terrible, as video game adaptations often are. Especially so for a game series I haven’t managed to bond with. But somehow it wasn’t terrible. It successfully communicated the quirks of vault dwellers and the sinister anarchy of the surface. Among everything else it felt fresh and entertaining. 
- [The Franchise](https://play.max.com/video/watch/90842edc-749f-4a73-81fd-433388c08c32): Want to watch the behind the scenes dumpster fire of Marvel movie making? That’s the concept of this show. It’s dry and funny. 
- [Ted Lasso: Season 3](https://tv.apple.com/us/show/ted-lasso/umc.cmc.vtoh0mn0xn7t3c643xqonfzy): If I’m being honest, I have a hard time here at the end of the year remembering specifically what happened in this final season of Ted Lasso. Loose ends got wrapped up, sure. But on the whole it continued the Lasso vibe. Positive, heartfelt, funny. What happened matters less than that. 

#### Maybe

- [Percy Jackson and the Olympians](https://ondisneyplus.disney.com/show/percy-jackson-and-the-olympians): The Percy Jackson movies were not great, either as adaptations or movies on their own. But now we get another attempt at bringing this popular book series to the screen, and this one fares so much better. It stays more faithful to the original book and the right age for the characters.
- [Avatar: the Last Airbender (live action)](https://www.netflix.com/title/80237957): I'm not sure who decided that a much beloved animated show needed a live action remake. It's not even a proper change of form like the [much maligned movie adaptation](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0938283/). This is still an episodic television series. I guess it's for people who can't take animation seriously? Why are we catering for such cretins? Anyway, for what it's worth this live action adaptation isn't bad. It's cast reasonably well, and hits many of the highlights of the original show. It's not better, it's certainly not quite as good, but I also don't regret watching it.
- [The Fall of the House of Usher](https://www.netflix.com/watch/81416384): This show follows in the horror series footsteps of the [Haunting of Hill House](https://www.netflix.com/title/80189221) and the [Haunting of Bly Manor](https://www.netflix.com/title/81237854), both of which are excellent. The Fall of the House of Usher is competent, but not nearly as good as its predecessors. It starts strong, but its rigid formula leads to it becoming predictable as it progresses through the episodes. Nothing quite takes the bite out of the tension like knowing what's going to happen.
