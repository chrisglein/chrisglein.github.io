A good year for board games. Convention-wise, I made it to OrcaCon in January, PAX West in September, and PAX Unplugged in November. Those are my biggest discovery times, but there was a steady drip of playing throughout the year.

Below are the board games I played in 2025 that I have something to say about.
<!--more-->

## [Sea Salt & Paper](https://boardgamegeek.com/boardgame/367220/sea-salt-and-paper)
[![Sea Salt & Paper](/media/posts/sea-salt-and-paper.jpg)](https://boardgamegeek.com/boardgame/367220/sea-salt-and-paper)
This is one of those games that looks like it’s going to be “pleasant” — and then it turns out to be *pleasantly sharp*. You’re assembling little sets of sea-themed cards (with lovely origami-style art), and the tension comes from timing: when do you cash out a decent hand versus push your luck one more draw? It’s quick, portable, and hits that sweet spot where you can teach it fast but still feel clever when you thread the needle.

<p class="playLine"><span class='playIcon bgg'></span>2-4 players, 30-45 minutes</p>

## [EGO](https://boardgamegeek.com/boardgame/429766/ego)
[![EGO](/media/posts/ego.jpg)](https://boardgamegeek.com/boardgame/429766/ego)
I only got this to the table twice, but it left a strong impression. It’s the kind of game that feels like a galactic road trip where you’re trying to look cool in front of aliens without accidentally insulting anyone important. There’s a lot of brinkmanship energy here — negotiating, vying for position, trying to read the table — and it rewards the kind of player who enjoys committing to a plan while staying flexible enough to pivot when the room shifts.

<p class="playLine"><span class='playIcon bgg'></span>2-5 players, 40-80 minutes</p>

## [Moon Colony Bloodbath](https://boardgamegeek.com/boardgame/425549/moon-colony-bloodbath)
[![Moon Colony Bloodbath](/media/posts/moon-colony-bloodbath.jpg)](https://boardgamegeek.com/boardgame/425549/moon-colony-bloodbath)
The title is doing a lot of work here, and I respect it for that. The pitch is basically: build a moon colony engine… and then watch it malfunction in increasingly hilarious ways. The central idea that grabbed me is that the deck is a shared ecosystem — you’re not just building *your* engine, you’re collectively building the machine that will later chew on everyone. It’s campy, mean in the fun way, and feels like it’s constantly one card away from everything going off the rails.

<p class="playLine"><span class='playIcon bgg'></span>1-5 players, 45-90 minutes</p>

## [Point Galaxy](https://boardgamegeek.com/boardgame/431998/point-galaxy)
[![Point Galaxy](/media/posts/point-galaxy.jpg)](https://boardgamegeek.com/boardgame/431998/point-galaxy)
The *Point* series continues to be a reliable “we have 20 minutes and want to feel smart” option. *Point Galaxy* is quick drafting with a satisfying sequencing puzzle: you’re building out little space-y sets and trying to position things so the scoring opportunities stack up instead of stepping on each other. It’s breezy, it moves, and it has that great quality where you can enjoy it casually — but if you want to get sweaty, you absolutely can.

<p class="playLine"><span class='playIcon bgg'></span>1-5 players, 15-30 minutes</p>

## And the Rest

Here are some other games I dug into this year. Some of these might warrant a stronger recommendation in the future but I don't have the repition of plays to back that up. Or they just might not be for everyone.

- [Castle Combo](https://boardgamegeek.com/boardgame/416851/castle-combo): A small-box tableau builder where the turns are simple and the payoff is all in how delightfully your grid of cards clicks together.
- [Pinched!](https://boardgamegeek.com/boardgame/450685/pinched): Heist-themed bluffing and deduction that feels like the table is constantly doing side-eye at each other (in the good way).
- [The Lord of the Rings: Duel for Middle-earth](https://boardgamegeek.com/boardgame/421006/the-lord-of-the-rings-duel-for-middle-earth): A strong two-player duel that feels like it’s constantly daring you to overcommit — and then punishing you when you do.
- [Rebirth](https://boardgamegeek.com/boardgame/417197/rebirth): Big friendly “modern euro” energy: clean decisions, satisfying placement, and a nice arc as the board fills in.

### Trick Taking

- [The Lord of the Rings: The Two Towers – Trick-Taking Game](https://boardgamegeek.com/boardgame/448271/the-lord-of-the-rings-the-two-towers-trick-taking): A clever continuation of the formula, with the same “co-op trick-taking as narrative” hook that makes this series feel like it shouldn’t work… but does.
- [The Lord of the Rings: The Fellowship of the Ring – Trick-Taking Game](https://boardgamegeek.com/boardgame/429293/the-lord-of-the-rings-the-fellowship-of-the-ring-t): The “wait, why is this so good?” entry point — a co-op campaign-ish trick taker that earns its novelty.
- [Fishing](https://boardgamegeek.com/boardgame/419195/fishing): Trick-taking, but your tricks turn into your future options. It’s a neat little loop that makes the hand feel alive over multiple rounds.
- [Dracula vs Van Helsing](https://boardgamegeek.com/boardgame/381278/dracula-vs-van-helsing): Asymmetric two-player tension in a compact box — quick turns, sharp interaction, and a theme that matches the mechanics.

### Meh

- [Cereal Killer](https://boardgamegeek.com/boardgame/449893/cereal-killer): A neat idea, but my first play didn’t find the fun — more “going through the motions” than “I need to play again immediately.”
- [The Tea Dragon Society Card Game](https://boardgamegeek.com/boardgame/236709/the-tea-dragon-society-card-game): Extremely cozy vibes, but the gameplay didn’t give me enough interesting decisions to justify the table time.
