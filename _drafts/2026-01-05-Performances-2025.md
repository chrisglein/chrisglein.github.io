2025 didn’t have a single gravitational-center show for me the way *Hadestown* did in 2023… but it *did* have range. A little swampy-electronic groove, a little virtuoso one-person-orchestra energy, some big glossy spectacle, and a couple of musicals that reminded me (again) how much live theater is a team sport.
<!--more-->

## Dirtwire
[![Dirtwire](/media/posts/dirtwire.jpg)](https://dirtwire.net/)
Dirtwire lives in that sweet spot between “this started on a front porch” and “this is powered by a spaceship.” It’s earthy and electronic at the same time, and it’s *extremely* my kind of nonsense. The best part is how the groove is doing two jobs at once: it’s the dance floor *and* the trance.

## Tash Sultana
[![Tash Sultana](/media/posts/tash-sultana.jpg)](https://www.tashsultana.com/)
There’s a special kind of satisfaction in watching somebody who is fully operating on their own internal compass. The whole show has that “I can build the world I want, live, in front of you” feel — looping, layering, shifting instruments, and making it look effortless even when it clearly isn’t.

## G. Love & Dispatch & John Butler
[![G. Love + Dispatch + John Butler](/media/posts/g-love-dispatch-john-butler.jpg)](https://dispatchmusic.com/)
This was a “three flavors, one night” kind of bill — which can either feel like a sampler platter or a chaotic buffet. This one landed on the good side of that line: different energies, but compatible.

G. Love brings that loose, friendly “we’re all hanging out” groove. Dispatch hits the nostalgia button in a way that still feels earned (and communal — you could *feel* how many people have lived with those songs). And John Butler is that rare performer where you can watch technique and emotion happen at the same time without one cancelling the other out.

## Whiskey Class
[![Whiskey Class](/media/posts/tony-a-fest.jpg)](/media/posts/tony-a-fest.jpg)
I love a DIY festival — the kind where the lineup feels curated by someone’s taste rather than an algorithm. The connective tissue matters here: the between-set reshuffling, the “you should listen to this person next” energy, the sense that the whole room is rooting for the whole bill.

## Cirque
[![Cirque](/media/posts/cirque.jpg)](/media/posts/cirque.jpg)
Cirque is basically a reliable machine for producing involuntary gasps. Every time I go to one of these I leave with the same thought: “Oh right, humans are *absurd*.”

## Stereophonic
[![Stereophonic](/media/posts/stereophonic.jpg)](https://stereophonicplay.com/)
This show is catnip if you love the *process* of making art more than the myth of making art. It drops you into that pressure-cooker space where creative collaboration turns into ego turns into vulnerability turns into something that might actually work.

It’s also one of those performances where you’re aware you’re holding your breath without noticing you started.

## Annie
[![Annie](/media/posts/annie.jpg)](https://www.mtishows.com/annie)
There are shows that feel like cultural furniture — they’ve been around long enough that even if you’ve never seen them, you’ve absorbed them through osmosis. *Annie* is that. And yes, the songs still hit that button in the brain that says “oh right, I know every word to this somehow.”

## Seussical
[![Seussical](/media/posts/seussical.jpg)](https://www.mtishows.com/seussical)
*Seussical* fully commits to being big, bright, and imaginative — but it’s also sneakily sincere. It’s nonsense with a spine. It’s one of those shows that feels like it was built to be seen with kids, but it’s secretly for the grown-ups too, because the whole thing is basically a reminder to take wonder seriously.
