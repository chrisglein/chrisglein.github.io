Bluffit

7.05172 ratings
2–6 playersAllow 30 minGerman MSRP: ≈ $9.85 USDBGG

Everyone secretly plays a numbered card, then claims a prize or challenges another claim. Spent cards become the next prizes, revealing which numbers are out of play.

Feels like: calling a bluff in Skull, except the spent cards give you evidence.

Challenges and the two-player reveal

Higher strength wins a challenge; ties favor the challenger. At two, subtract your own card from the new prize pool and you know exactly what your opponent spent.

Snowp

7.6175 ratings
Plays at twoAllow 45 minJapan MSRP: ≈ $34.47 USDBGG📄 Rulebook

Flick rings around colored bells to collect sets. The last color left on the board scores nothing, so sinking an easy shot can wipe out your best set.

Feels like: aiming a shot in Crokinole, but with a reason to leave certain targets alone.

When the easy shot costs points

Your biggest set is blue, and only blue and yellow bells remain. Clear the last yellow bell and your blues score nothing. Score over three rounds, carrying a limited number of bells forward.

Fadenfroh

7.57160 ratings
1–4 playersAllow 60 minUS MSRP: $29.95BGG📄 Rulebook (German)

Draft supplies and overlap fabric cards to stitch a scoring pattern. Taking thread keeps a player in the draft; claiming fabric ends their shopping. The last shopper gets the leftover materials.

Feels like: the Azul draft dilemma: what will taking this leave for the next player?

Shopping, stitching and the needle exception

The five-round loop

  1. Each shopping turn, take one material (spool, needle, or thimble) and stay in, or take a fabric offer and finish shopping for that round.
  2. The last shopper gets the remaining materials before taking the remaining offer. Waiting isn't automatically bad.
  3. In stitching, every set of three spools must become a draw-three/keep-one fabric choice. Normally a new fabric card overlaps at least half of an existing card.
  4. A needle allows edge-to-edge placement without covering half a card. Score the round's indicated objectives; there is no scoring in round one.

The actual puzzle

What you cover matters as much as what you add. A card may improve this round's colors while hiding symbols needed next round. The scoring schedule is visible: prepare for the next pair, not just the current one.

A rule worth remembering

Spools convert in groups of three during stitching; this isn't an optional currency to stockpile indefinitely. Unplaced fabric cannot be saved for later rounds. Needles and up to two spools can remain.

Drops of Evil

7.78158 ratings
2–4 playersAllow 90 minUS MSRP: $60BGG📄 Rulebook

Worker placement followed by a potion puzzle. Send animal familiars into a shared forest: the crow grabs one ingredient early, while the snake covers more ground but arrives last. Fit what comes home into vials.

Feels like: a race at the shared board followed by a packing puzzle in your own cauldron.

Familiar turn order and the vial-versus-column puzzle

Three rounds, four phases

  1. Place five action pawns: four different familiars and a coin pouch for an assistant. The familiars collect different footprints of forest spaces.
  2. Gather in a fixed order: Crows, Cats, Toads, then Snakes. Empty spaces give a worm instead of the resources someone already collected.
  3. Simultaneously arrange the haul in your Alchilab. Ingredients fill vials, mushrooms drive the distiller, and rats need handling.
  4. Gain income from revealed icons and lose points for uncaptured rats. After round three, add full-vial, captured-rat, and recipe scoring.

Two shapes of progress

Vials: one ingredient type per vial, with spiders as wilds; full vials score that ingredient's toxicity at the end. Columns: filling a vertical column across vials unlocks immediate bonuses and potentially income. A placement can help one goal while making the other harder.

Incomplete vials score nothing, even when their ingredients have earned a column bonus.

Bologna

A two-player area-majority game on a shared city map. Place towers to control districts at game end, or enclose courtyards for influence and a chance to win immediately.

Feels like: closing a city in Carcassonne, but here the enclosure can win the duel outright.

Upside Town

8.3046 ratings
1–4 playersAllow 60 minBGG📄 Rulebook

Stack magnetic building pieces to match the skylines on contract cards. You keep rebuilding the same city, so a good stack scores now and comes apart neatly for the next contract.

Feels like: the hands-on, three-dimensional pattern building in Harmonies or Reef.

Which buildings attract or repel?

Know this before sitting down

  1. Each player has five building sites and storage for four units. Units are one, two, or three levels tall.
  2. Take two main actions: obtain a single, upgrade, split, build, demolish, or take a contract. You may repeat an action.
  3. Match a contract's skyline heights to score it for free. Its rewards can help fund the next skyline; free construction tokens save actions.
  4. Magnetic polarity restricts what can stack. Identical doubles repel; identical triples repel. Singles can be flipped to bridge the problem.

Two towers of the same height can take different numbers of actions to rebuild. A single you can flip is more flexible than a double locked into the stack.

Queen Alice

8.191,244 ratings
1–4 playersAllow 120 minSpain MSRP: ≈ $76.70 USDBGG📄 Rulebook

A Wonderland euro where a shared chessboard selects your actions. Discard one card for its reward, then play another to move a piece: its destination determines what you gain and which scoring puzzle you can work on.

Feels like: a long puzzle night rather than a chess duel. A knight’s move can put a book or a rose within reach.

The two-card turn and how Wishes score

The turn in plain English

  1. Discard one hand card for its immediate reward. Play a second card to use the indicated chess piece.
  2. Move that piece using its chess movement, or rescue it from jail. The landing row gives a resource; the column determines the action. You can use both colors.
  3. Pay the action's cost and resolve its bonuses. You may spend six Time to repeat that action in the same color, paying its costs again.
  4. Refill your hand, refresh the market and used bookmarks, and resolve any Wish scoring triggered this turn.

The four engines

Hearts: rosebushes and permanent flower powers. Spades: improve your deck or "invite" cards out of it for scoring. Clubs: gather books and buy bookmarks. Diamonds: build the wall for rewards and progress. The shared board adds tactical opportunities; it isn't checkmate.

Don't fall into this trap

Fulfilling a Wish's condition isn't enough for endgame scoring: your Cat-track progress controls access. Coronation upgrades the winning color's Wishes and affects crown points. The last available crown triggers the finish, so a slow start can accelerate sharply.

Frosted Blooms

7.441,332 ratings
1–4 playersAllow 60 minUS MSRP: $34.99BGG📄 Rulebook

Draft polyomino tiles to grow a tulip garden, then play a card to score what you just planted. Leave little enclosed gaps for structures instead of filling every square.

Feels like: fitting pieces in Patchwork while drafting toward a pattern, as in Cascadia.

When a hole scores more than a flower bed

The ten-turn arc

  1. Draft from a circular market. The next tile is free; pay a coin onto each tile you skip and collect coins on the one you take.
  2. Place the tile orthogonally adjacent to your garden. Rotate or flip it; matching terrain is desirable, not a placement requirement.
  3. Play one scoring card. It scores matching elements in the newly placed tile and the relevant connected group.
  4. Enclose small gaps for structures and compete for common objectives. Each player has ten scoring cards for ten turns, so the hand's timing matters.

The windmill tradeoff

The tension is grow a connected scoring patch versus leave precisely the right empty space. A closed 2-by-2 hole gets a windmill worth 25 endgame points. Your current scoring card may instead reward extending the patch. The choice is about both geometry and timing.

The Glasgow Train Robbery

7.82184 ratings
Exactly 2 / cooperativeAllow 120 minUK RRP: ≈ $45.93 USDBGG📄 RulebookEnglish PDF

An asymmetric co-op heist for two: one of you prepares the crew, the other runs the job. Play cards to finish five plans before the train arrives; your actions advance the clock, and talking costs you.

Feels like: the communication squeeze of Codenames: Duet. Here you can spend an action to say a few words, but the train keeps moving.

Prep: Have the teacher run through one complete rest before play; it resets more than your hand.

Who handles each plan, and when you can talk

The collaboration

  1. The Coordinator manages the cottage and crew preparation; the Operator manages field operatives and contingencies. The Operator starts.
  2. On your turn, play an action card, resolve a qualifying contingency (Operator), complete a qualifying plan, or rest.
  3. Playing an action card advances the train by its clock symbols before you choose one of the card's actions.
  4. Complete five plans: the Coordinator handles plans 1-4; the Operator handles plan 5. Beat the train and avoid defeat from accumulated clues.

Not "we'll just talk less"

Free strategic discussion about tools, destinations, or plans is forbidden. A communication action permits two or three keywords according to its value. Actions, equipment transfers, movement, and rest timing also communicate intention indirectly.

Playing a card as an event removes it from the game, along with all its other actions.

Greylune

7.65538 ratings
2–4 playersAllow 120 minUS MSRP: $39.95BGG📄 RulebookEnglish PDF

Place villagers between buildings, then pick them back up to earn money or use a building. You decide when to switch from placing to collecting: a crowd pays better but makes the action pricier.

Feels like: a game of chicken over a crowded shop. Cash out now or wait for the next villager to raise the price?

Leaving Spring early and counting the crowd

What's genuinely unusual

  1. Over five years, the village is rebuilt as a three-by-three card grid. Place villagers between adjacent cards in Spring, not directly on them.
  2. Choose when to move to Summer. This is irreversible for the year; others may keep placing while you begin retrieving.
  3. Retrieve a villager either to gain one coin per other worker around a chosen adjacent card, or activate that card at its price plus the same crowd surcharge.
  4. Buy items and companions, adventure through encounters, and tell completed encounters as stories for rewards. Companions grant a worker but reduce Autumn income.

Grinivil

8.08278 ratings
2–6 playersAllow 180 minBGG

An economic city builder on one shared map. Buy land, lay roads and construct buildings to attract residents. Several players can meet the same resident’s needs and split the rewards.

Feels like: planning neighborhoods in Suburbia, except your new road can increase the value of a rival’s building.

Prep: Before choosing your first plot, have the teacher walk through one Time Action Ring move and a resident payout split between two builders.

The Time Action Ring and shared resident payouts

The system to understand

  1. The Time Action Ring controls action choice and pace. Actions consume different amounts of time; efficient loops are part of the economic puzzle.
  2. Buy land, connect roads, plant trees, and build residences and civic/commercial facilities in a shared town.
  3. Residents act like contracts with mandatory and optional requirements. Several players may contribute to satisfying them.
  4. Payouts reflect those different contributions; resident conditions also matter at endgame. A useful building for the town may reward another player more than you.

Secretly divide your cards among three objectives. Two wins take the sector, but each objective offers its own reward. Which one can you afford to concede?

Feels like: Reading a facedown commitment in Netrunner. Play one sector to see whether you can call the bluff.

Joinery

Rock Manor · 2363 · 1–5 players · 90–120 min

Fill a woodworking contract and spend the proceeds on tools for more demanding jobs.

Feels like: Filling orders in Century: Spice Road, but here the payoff changes your workshop. Follow one project through to see what the upgrade lets you build next.

The Last Command: Advanced Warfare

Around the Stump · 4327 · 1–2 players · 30–60 min

Each captain runs a starship with separate crew and systems decks. Shields you don’t spend become power next turn.

Feels like: A duel where staying on defense can fund your next attack. It’s an expansion demo; ask whether the teach starts from scratch.

Monster Academy: Hexes & Hijinks

Calliope · 1951 · 1–6 players · 40–60 min

Defend a school together. Pick one of three cards: act now, lay a trap, or set up a teammate.

Feels like: A shared tactical puzzle. Choose it over the nearby crew draft if you’d rather plan the next move together.

All True Believers

Pillbox · 3352 · 4–10 players · 45–90 min

One player offers two cards in secret; a second chooses which goes on the mission. When a mission fails, either of them could have sabotaged it.

Feels like: A hidden-role accusation with a specific card choice to argue about. Join a group for this one.

Rolliverse

Ravensburger · 3227

Roll five dice up to three times. Keep a combination to claim matching cards from your hand and the shared display, or reroll for a bigger haul.

Feels like: Yahtzee with the prizes face up. A lighter stop between longer teaches.

Compile: Main 1

Luma · 2643 · 2 players · 20–30 min

Two rival AIs stack cards in three lanes. Compile a protocol by building up enough value while keeping the opposing stack smaller.

Feels like: A tug-of-war where covering a card can switch off its power. Ask to try the standard box; the tournament uses an event booster.

Lords of Vegas

Medium Brow · 4307 · 2–4 players · 60–90 min

Build casinos together, but compete to be the boss. A new lot can merge neighboring casinos; a reroll can put somebody else in charge.

Feels like: A property deal that can turn on a die. Ask for the base game, listed separately from Deluxe.

Oath

Buried Giant · 2263 · 1–6 players · 45–150 min

The Chancellor tries to hold the realm while exiles challenge the terms of victory. Whoever wins shapes the next game’s starting position.

Feels like: A political negotiation that doesn’t reset when you pack up. Ask if the base game is set up rather than an expansion.
